About

Deseret is a more efficient, learnable and aesthetically pleasing spelling system for the English language. It was created in the 1850s under the leadership of Brigham Young.

Each letter stands for one sound, so a word is spelled the way it is said. There are no silent letters and no competing spellings for the same sound. Capital and small forms differ only in size.

History

English spelling had troubled reformers long before the 1850s. Its rules record centuries of sound change, borrowing and printers' habit rather than the language as it is spoken, and learning to read means learning each word twice: once as sound, once as spelling.

The Board of Regents

The alphabet was developed at the University of Deseret in Salt Lake City from 1853, by a committee of the Board of Regents working under Brigham Young. The most active hand was George D. Watt, who had trained in Pitman shorthand and brought a phonographer's instincts to the problem: one symbol per sound, and no sound without a symbol.

The result was a wholly new set of letterforms rather than an adaptation of the Latin ones. That was deliberate. A reader who cannot fall back on familiar shapes cannot fall back on familiar spellings either.

In print

Type was cast in the 1860s, and the Deseret News printed occasional passages. Four books followed: the First and Second Books in 1868, and in 1869 a Book of Mormon and a third reader. Little else was ever set in it.

Why it stopped

The obstacles were practical rather than linguistic. New type was expensive, and every book in existence was already in the Latin alphabet; a reader fluent in Deseret would have had almost nothing to read. Most adults could already read English perfectly well and had no reason to start again. Brigham Young's death in 1877 removed the project's remaining institutional support.

Since

The alphabet was encoded in Unicode 3.1 in 2001, in the range U+10400–U+1044F, which is what makes this page possible: the letters below are text, not images. Fonts, keyboards and converters have followed. The barrier that stopped it in 1877 — the cost of new type — no longer exists.

Syllabic values

Letters of the Deseret Alphabet may have names that are sounded when the letter is used by itself. 𐐻 has the name “tee”, so it can stand alone for the word tea; likewise 𐑅 for es and 𐑊 for el.
